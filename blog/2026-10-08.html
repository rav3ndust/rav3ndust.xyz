modes of operation

pick up the pencil when it makes sense

2026-10-08

A few weeks ago at the Rails World conference, DHH (of Omarchy and 37signals fame) declared it was time for "pencils down".

If you aren't in the loop, what this is referencing is programmers no longer doing the majority of code-writing by hand. Largely, this is becoming truer and truer. We are seeing many agent-driven projects coming along faster than ever before, and more and more people (including me!) working directly with agents to accelerate the development of their software.

I like DHH, and I like agents. I have agents on my local navi machines that I run through ollama. If I need something a little more powerful, I reach for an open model through Ollama's Cloud. It is a very fun, very good workflow. While I work a lot with agents, I don't think I'm going to be fully pencils-down anytime soon, because I still also love the joy of writing by hand as well. For example, this very website has a very explicit rule - it will always be handwritten, with no agents touching it. Our main agent, lain, has its own website, and it is, of course, fully agent-driven. I have not written a single line of code for that site, except for creating the repo for it. That's because that site is a fun experiment: What happens if you hand an agent an empty GitHub repo and a domain?

Instead of going fully "pencils-down", here at xvoidsx, we function in a different fashion, which is through different modes of operation. Let's take a little look at what I mean by that.


the three "modes" at xvoidsx

Over the last little while, as we have finally been pushing out ISOs for navi, our GNU/Linux distro (which I will be doing a more thorough write-up on later in this blog and in the wiki), I have identified the three main ways we tend to function around here.

the takeaways

So, to continue with the pencil analogy, is picking up the pencil now a dead practice?

I think it largely depends on the project, personally. You're going to have loads of "artisan" projects that refuse any agent commits in them, at all, now and in the future, most likely. There are always going to be anti-AI people, at least in our own lifetimes. Maybe they will become less and less as time marches on and this new AI-accelerated world becomes more the norm. Right now, it is still quite the new concept, no matter how long like it feels it's been around, in reality, it's only been a few years, and the technology only continues to get better and better.

While I am not a fan of the corporate-backed AI platforms for their potential privacy issues, I do like the technology as a whole, and I very much enjoy doing stuff with open-weights models. I run them myself, as I mentioned earlier, through Ollama, and I like checking out other models through Ollama's cloud. A lot of the open harnesses, such as OpenCode (which we made a fork for with our own setup inside, called Wisp for navi), Hermes, Pi, and others, are very good for doing stuff with agents. The herdr multiplexer which doubles as a sort of runtime for agents is another great tool in the toolbox, an agent-aware tmux of sorts that shows you agents' working state, whether or not your agents need your attention, and stuff like that, is genuinely good, and something else we ship in navi.
